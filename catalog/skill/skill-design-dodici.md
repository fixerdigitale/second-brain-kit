# The 12 design skills and the brief that names them all

**The problem.** Pages made by the AI all looked alike and had the same flaws: wrong spacing, flat hierarchy, weak contrast. And an agent that says it "used the skills" has sometimes opened two of them.

**How I solved it.** I compared several design skills on the same page, with separate reviewers and one without skills as a control. I kept 12, all third-party: impeccable, design-critic, design-taste-frontend, refactoring-ui, critique-visual-hierarchy, the five better-* ones, ui-ux-pro-max and hallmark. My piece is the brief template: it names them all, asks for a starting visual reference and, in the delivery, one line per skill (opened yes/no, what it changed). Then a blind review.

**Why this way.** The test showed that no skill invents a new composition on its own: you need a starting direction and a check. The skills are for finding and fixing flaws. The line per skill shows me right away who really read them. Of hallmark and ui-ux-pro-max I use only the texts, not the scripts.

**When you need it.** If you have the AI make pages, landing pages or interfaces and want a level above the "AI template".

**When you DON'T need it.** If you have a designer who decides, or an already settled design system: its rules are enough. For a quick internal page 12 skills are too many: refactoring-ui and better-layout are enough.

**How to get it.** The skills come from the sources listed in the package guide (among them pbakaus/impeccable, edisonmbli/refactoring-ui-skill, jakubkrehel/skills, nextlevelbuilder/ui-ux-pro-max-skill, Nutlope/hallmark; design-critic has its source to be verified).
