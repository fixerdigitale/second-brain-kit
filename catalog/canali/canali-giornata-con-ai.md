# My day with AI

**The problem.** I used to use AI only when I opened a chat. Everything else (remembering who's waiting, what came out of a call, what was left half done) stayed on my shoulders, and in the evening my head was full of loose threads.

**How I solved it.** Today the day has a fixed rhythm, almost all automatic.

- **At night**, between one in the morning and dawn, in a row: I save versions of pages and documents; data backup; a run that proposes skill improvements by reading the sessions (I adopt them); encrypted copy to Drive; collection of facts from chats and voice notes into the vault, and a reviewer that rereads my corrections of the day; memory tidy-up (stable facts move from the work notes to the fixed sheets); inventory of what exists on the server; repeated corrections become "make it a permanent rule?" cards, which I apply only with my ok.
- **Early morning.** First run of my agentic PM: it reads the sessions and the mail and updates the tasks, without sending me anything. Then the state collection (calls, tasks, calendar, sessions) and, Monday to Friday, the brief on WhatsApp before I start working.
- **During the day.** I work between Claude Code in VS Code, the WhatsApp chat with my main assistant (voice notes too) and claude.ai on the phone. A tag on Slack or WhatsApp is an event: it arrives right away, not on a schedule. A finished call becomes a note with draft tasks by itself. Requests for my ok wait for me on a single page. If I've been idle for a while and the plan has capacity that would be lost, a work shift starts without me. More PM runs and more collections around midday and in the afternoon. On Friday morning the PM also does the weekly round.
- **Evening and night.** Silence, except for something explicitly written as urgent.

**Why this way.** My limit isn't doing things, it's keeping them in mind. This way the memory lives in the system: in the morning I already know what matters, during the day I decide, at night the part that doesn't need me moves forward by itself. Fixed times help because I know when what arrives, and I don't keep checking.

**When you need it.** If you work with several clients at once, forget who's waiting for an answer, or have AI plan capacity that goes unused at the end of the week.

**When you DON'T need it.** If you have one or two projects and your day fits in a handwritten list, it's too much: the morning brief run by hand is enough. And without a server you don't get the night part and the real-time alerts this way: on a PC they only run while the PC is on.

**How to get it.** This is the story of the whole system, not a package.
