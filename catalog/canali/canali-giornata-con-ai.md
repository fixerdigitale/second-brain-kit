# My day with AI

**The problem.** I used to use AI only when I opened a chat. Everything else (remembering who's waiting, what came out of a call, what was left half done) stayed on my shoulders, and in the evening my head was full of loose threads.

**How I solved it.** Today the day has a fixed rhythm, almost all automatic. Times are Italian time.

- **At night.** 01:50 I save versions of pages and documents; 02:15 data backup; 03:30 a run that proposes skill improvements by reading the sessions (I adopt them); 03:45 encrypted copy to Drive; 04:00 collection of facts from chats and voice notes into the vault, and a reviewer that rereads my corrections of the day; 04:40 memory tidy-up (stable facts move from the work notes to the fixed sheets); 05:35 inventory of what exists on the server; 06:30 repeated corrections become "make it a permanent rule?" cards, which I apply only with my ok.
- **Morning.** 07:00 first run of my agentic PM: it reads the sessions and the mail and updates the tasks, without sending me anything. 07:40 state collection (calls, tasks, calendar, sessions). 08:00, Monday to Friday, the brief on WhatsApp.
- **During the day.** I work between Claude Code in VS Code, the WhatsApp chat with my main assistant (voice notes too) and claude.ai on the phone. A tag on Slack or WhatsApp is an event: it arrives right away, not on a schedule. A finished call becomes a note with draft tasks by itself. Requests for my ok wait for me on a single page. Every 15 minutes, if I've been idle for 45 and the plan has capacity that would be lost, a work shift starts without me. More PM runs at 12, 15 and 18; more collections at 13:05 and 19:05. On Friday at 7 the PM also does the weekly round.
- **Evening.** From 23 to 8, silence, except for something explicitly written as urgent.

**Why this way.** My limit isn't doing things, it's keeping them in mind. This way the memory lives in the system: in the morning I already know what matters, during the day I decide, at night the part that doesn't need me moves forward by itself. Fixed times help because I know when what arrives, and I don't keep checking.

**When you need it.** If you work with several clients at once, forget who's waiting for an answer, or have AI plan capacity that goes unused at the end of the week.

**When you DON'T need it.** If you have one or two projects and your day fits in a handwritten list, it's too much: the morning brief run by hand is enough. And without a server you don't get the night part and the real-time alerts this way: on a PC they only run while the PC is on.

**How to get it.** This is the story of the whole system, not a package.
